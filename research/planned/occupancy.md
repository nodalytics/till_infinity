# Occupancy: where price spends time, and what counting statistics say about it

**Status: not measured.** None of this exists in the code.

Two proposals arrive together and turn out to be one measurement: **levels from the density of the
price cloud** rather than from its turns, and **quantum statistics** as a lens on how visits
distribute. The second is the formal version of the first, and there is a trap in it that would
otherwise manufacture a result.

## The two constructions, and why they ask different questions

Everything in this codebase draws levels from **turns**. `pips.py` keeps the points furthest from
the line joining their neighbours; `runs`, `origin`, `sweeps` and `equals` all locate a place where
price *changed direction*. [`pip-windows.md`](../docs/pip-windows.md) measured whether several
window lengths sharpen that and found they do not - 99.4% redundancy with the interval ladder and a
flat reaction rate across 130,000 touches.

A **density** level is a different object: not where price turned, but where it **spent time**.
Treat the lookback closes as a one-dimensional point cloud and emit the local maxima of its density
as levels, scored by how dense the peak is.

Partly covered already - `profile.py` builds a volume profile, which is a density weighted by
volume. What is not covered is the unweighted version, and the difference matters: a volume profile
asks where business was done, a plain density asks where price *lingered*, and on this broker's
synthetics there is no real volume at all, only tick count.

Three input modes worth separating, because they are three different claims: **closes** (where it
settled), **full OHLC** (where it traded), **wicks only** (where it was rejected from). The wick
variant is the one with the clearest story - `pips.extremes` exists for exactly the argument that a
swing high is a high and not a close.

## Quantum statistics is the right frame, and it names the null

The question behind every level in this repository is: **does price return to a level more often
than chance?** That is an occupancy problem, and occupancy is where counting statistics lives.

Bin price into a grid of cells. Each visit is a particle, each cell a state. The occupancy
distribution then distinguishes:

* **Maxwell-Boltzmann** - distinguishable, independent placements, giving Poisson occupancy;
* **Bose-Einstein** - indistinguishable with multiple occupancy allowed, giving a geometric,
  over-dispersed occupancy with far more heavily-visited cells than Poisson permits;
* **Fermi-Dirac** - at most one per cell, under-dispersed. Not a plausible market model, but worth
  stating so the three-way comparison is complete rather than a two-way one dressed up.

Measured over-dispersion against Poisson would say price clusters its visits - the weak form of
"levels are real".

### The trap, which is the whole reason to write this down first

**A random walk is already Bose-Einstein-like, and testing against Poisson would find "clustering"
in pure Brownian motion.**

The reason is local time. A walk that reaches a level tends to cross it repeatedly before escaping
the neighbourhood - the number of returns before departure is approximately geometric, which is
precisely the Bose-Einstein occupancy form. So a naive test comparing real price visits to a Poisson
null is guaranteed to reject the null, on any series, including one generated by a coin.

The correct null is therefore **not Poisson**. It is the occupancy distribution of a driftless walk
with the same volatility and the same number of bars, obtained by simulation or by block-shuffling
the increments. Only an excess over *that* is evidence of anything.

This is the same error shape as three others already recorded here: `norm_variance`'s slope
predicted at 1.0 and measured at 0.784, `premium_discount` tracking `a / (a + b)` because a rule
placing stop and target at range edges has its hit rate *determined* by geometry, and
[`costs.md`](../docs/costs.md)'s bar-extreme slippage coming out four times the realised figure.
Each was a null chosen for convenience rather than for correctness.

### What a real result would look like

Levels whose occupancy exceeds the matched-walk null, **and** where the excess predicts a reaction
that clears the cost hurdle. The second half is not optional: `imbalance.md` found gap edges beating
a matched placebo by +0.6% and still losing to the cost line, so an occupancy excess that does not
convert is a distributional curiosity.

The detection floor comes first, per [`power-and-sizing.md`](../docs/power-and-sizing.md): a
2.0-point hurdle on FX needs about 5,000 resolved touches per bucket, which the touch counts in
`pip_levels.py` clear comfortably at 99,000.

## Three more ideas from the same source, filtered on the sensor test

**Imbalance and its rate of change.** The claim: determine whether the market is tilted to buyers
or sellers, and use the *derivative* of that tilt - a strong shift to enter, an unsustainable one to
exit. The derivative is the specific and interesting part; the level of imbalance is what everyone
computes.

True order-flow imbalance needs depth the bridge does not serve, so this is blocked in its proper
form. But a bar-only proxy exists and has never been built: `Series.volumes` carries tick count per
bar, and signing it by candle direction gives a crude cumulative delta. That is a genuinely
different quantity from anything measured this session - it reads *how much activity* accompanied a
move, not just the move - so it passes the sensor test even in proxy form. The honest caveat is
already written down in `context/activity.py`: on most feeds here volume is tick count rather than
size, and spot FX has no consolidated volume at all, so this is a weight within one series and
never a comparison across instruments.

**Profit checkpoints.** Re-evaluate at predetermined profit levels and decide whether the position
is still sustainable, closing partially if not. **This is the most actionable idea in any of these
sources**, because it attacks the one verified live defect: [`excursion.md`](../docs/excursion.md)
records a median capture of 0.698 of favourable excursion and eight trades that reached 1R or better
and still lost, costing -5.01R between them.

And it survives the objection that killed the obvious fix. [`breakout-runs.md`](../docs/breakout-runs.md)
showed excursion is exponential with mean equal to the giveback, so widening or narrowing a trailing
stop rescales the run and the captured *fraction* is invariant - there is no trail width to tune. A
checkpoint is not a trail: it is a state-dependent decision at a fixed profit, which the
scale-invariance argument does not cover. That makes it the one exit change with a live defect to
fix and no measured reason it cannot work.

**Echo state networks and hidden Markov regimes.** Deferred, and the reason is
[`more-mathematics.md`](more-mathematics.md): both are learned representations over the same close
series, whose effective dimension is about five. They become interesting downstream of a signal,
and [`reservoirs.md`](../docs/reservoirs.md) already exists here.

## One note on the six-layer scorecard

That source grades itself across six layers - prove the signal, know the tail, size to survive it,
compound across years, stay disciplined, update on decay - and gives itself a **C on compounding**,
with the note that it is "believed, not earned".

Worth recording because this session independently measured the same thing from the other end.
`power-and-sizing.md` found the live book's realised edge negative at -0.0757 R a trade, so the
growth-optimal fraction is zero: there is nothing to compound yet. The two assessments agree, and
the honest reading is that layers one and five are where the work has actually been done, which is
also what the measured nulls in this repository show.
